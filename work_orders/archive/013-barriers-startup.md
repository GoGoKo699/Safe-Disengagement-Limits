# Pass 13: rate barriers, large deadlines, and paid startup

22 September 2026. Passes 11–12 establish unequal-rate concentration and an
explicit cold-prefix frontier. Their original orders are archived. The numeric
reference checks for pass 12 are still being completed alongside this pass.

## Question

When some preparation rates are inaccessible before another module transfers,
can a sufficiently long deadline remove all recurring upkeep? Distinguish the
answer with paid initialization from what can be reached in finite normal
warmup from cold.

## Tasks

1. Characterize finite exit using closure of the initially full set under the
   strict threshold b(S)>gamma_i*M_i. Prove that partial preparation changes
   finite completion times but cannot cross an inaccessible-rate barrier.
2. Derive the exact minimum upkeep floor over successful initially full seeds,
   and determine whether the floor is reached at a finite deadline.
3. If the completely cold state has no finite exit, classify which initialized
   indefinite policies remain possible within the normal spare budget. Test
   whether finite cold normal warmup can ever reach a deadline-ready state.
4. Prove a finite warmup construction when C(H)<s. Keep C(H)=s cases separate;
   do not infer their reachability merely from stationary feasibility.
5. Record exact threshold checks separately from floating deadline calculations.
   Assess the result as a consequence of rate barriers, without promoting
   ordinary monotone closure or storage averaging to a new method.

Continue after recording the result. The next worthwhile question is the
critical equality C(H)=s when cold exit is finite: establish a sharp startup
criterion in the smallest nontrivial case, or retain a precise open boundary.
The central paper candidate remains proportional concentration and its nonlinear
failure, subject to the source and operational comparisons in the latest notes.
