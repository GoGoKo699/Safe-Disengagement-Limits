# Pass 27: quantify the small-mismatch boundary

23 September 2026. Completed follow-on to the common-drain theorem; this
order records its question and outcome retrospectively. Repository research
only; preserve manuscript and earlier notes.

Does two-module concentration failure require a fixed positive difference
between drain durations? Test an exact family with all other physical
parameters fixed. Require bounds over both possible handoff orders, matching
robust attainment, and comparison with every concentrated state. Preserve
positive preparation tolerance only where the caps actually permit it.
Distinguish an arbitrarily small absolute mismatch from instability around
a fixed nondegenerate common-drain instance.

Outcome: [the complete family](../../research/2026-09-23-pass27-small-drain-mismatch.md)
has a unique two-partial optimum for every positive parameter in its domain.
Its concentration penalty is exact and quadratic to leading order in the
drain mismatch. Its certified tolerance, optional slack and preparation
windows vanish in the common-drain limit. The result does not claim a
constant advantage or fixed positive tolerance as mismatch vanishes.
The [assessment](../../research/2026-09-23-pass26-28-assessment.md) also shows
that padding both durations to one common bound costs exactly this penalty
at zero preparation deficit.

The next justified step is the general pair-order consequence already
present in the all-policy perturbation, not a numerical scheduling search.
