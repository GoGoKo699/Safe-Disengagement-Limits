# Pass 20 independent mathematical review

23 September 2026. Repository research only. This review concerns the full
pass-19 concentration proof, the pass-20 completion-time opening, and the
new geometry and nested-allocation notes. It is an independent internal
audit, not external certification or a literature-wide priority verdict.
No manuscript file was edited or built for this review.

## 1. The strengthened proof remains sound

The pass-19 proof correctly uses one attaining strict order. Its two-variable
variation preserves the completion time of the second selected stage and
therefore preserves the whole suffix; it does not differentiate the minimum
over orders. Positive interiors of the two selected caps supply an open
variation interval, and the support charges remain constant there.

For each fixed-preparation stage, the displayed derivative is positive and
the second derivative is nonpositive on every nonnegative start time.
Composition preserves these properties. The tangent inequality at zero
indeed yields `G(t)>=t*G'(t)`. With `b_k>=b_j>D_j`, the stationary equation
cannot hold when `gamma_k*G'(t)>=gamma_j`. Substitution into the second
derivative therefore gives strict negativity, including when some or all
intermediate maps are affine. This excludes every interior local minimum,
which is the strict statement needed for every-minimizer concentration.

The conversion from a fixed positive nominal shortfall to reduced caps and
positive support charges is exact. In particular, concentration concerns
the nominal design, not the physical fullness of the shortfall state.
No flaw was found in these quantifiers or endpoint conventions.

## 2. Positive geometry reductions checked

The completion-time inversion and its linked objective telescope correctly.
The common exponential clock `z_k=exp(lambda*t_k)` with
`lambda>=max gamma_k` gives an exact convex domain. I recomputed its lower
graph's second derivative; its sign is nonnegative, and strict when the
cap is positive and its rate is below `lambda`. Thus a blanket assertion
that the timing problem cannot be convexified would be false.

The geometry note's common-rate raw-time counterexample has a genuinely
infeasible midpoint. It is properly restricted to those coordinates and
does not contradict the known common-rate linear allocation reduction.

The unequal-rate Hessian fixture was checked by substituting all three
preparations into the stage recurrences. Both Hessian signs are correct,
and the strict cap interiors give the relative open feasible neighborhood
needed for the nonconvex/nonconcave objective statement. The note correctly
does not infer failure for every possible clock from one valid clock.

The pass-19 three-stage fixture is also an extreme point of its full convex
timing domain: an active terminal deadline fixes that coordinate in a
convex decomposition; strict convexity of the active middle lower graph
then fixes the first two coordinates. The first and third preparations are
nevertheless interior. The exact decreasing exchange from pass 19 proves
this point is not a minimum. Therefore a generic extreme-point existence
result alone does not imply the claimed preparation pattern.

For a fixed nonincreasing-rate order, the stage-dependent recursion is
concave in preparation. The single reverse-convex deadline and box-section
vertex argument do establish existence of a concentrated minimum, including
nonnegative support charges. They do not establish that all minima
concentrate, nor that a best S1 order must have nonincreasing rates. These
positive reductions should remain in the current attribution assessment.

## 3. Equal-amount exchange certificate checked against the source

I independently opened the primary
[Schoot Uiterkamp–Gerards–Hurink preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.11829)
and inspected its visible Section 2 definitions and Section 3 Condition 1,
Theorem 1, and complete proof. The theorem permits general nonempty feasible
sets but requires its stated exchange optimality condition. An exchangeable
pair uses some positive amount, not merely an infinitesimal direction.
Thus nonconvexity alone would not exclude that theorem.

For the nested-allocation note's exact preparation-plus-slack set, all six
directed exchange classifications at Q are correct. In particular, the
excluded `x`-to-`y` exchange fails for every box-admissible positive amount:
the squared deadline discrepancy is `delta*(9-delta)>0`. Each of the three
allowed directions has a sufficiently small feasible step and nonnegative
objective derivative.

The lower-cost point P is feasible. I checked both its linked linear cost
decrease and the shared quadratic construction. The latter has gradient
`(2,1,0)` at Q and the exact improvement `-1299/400000` at P. This falsifies
the required sufficient optimality condition for this displayed fixed-order
set, including after adding the natural constant-total slack coordinate.
It does not falsify the cited theorem, whose hypothesis is absent here.

The restriction to this fixed order and these variables matters: no result
has been proved about the condition after taking a union over orders or
under arbitrary nonlinear encodings. The note states these limits. Its
separate Vidal–Gribel–Jaillet inspection record was reviewed for consistency
with the algebra, but that source was not independently re-read in this
review; the source-comparison author records the actual sections inspected.

## 4. Review decision and next mathematical boundary

No mathematical correction is required in the audited pass-20 notes. The
comparison excludes specified direct substitutions and preserves useful
restricted reductions. It leaves the general attribution question open.
It would be unjustified to turn these certificates into a claim that no
prior allocation theorem can absorb the strengthened result.

The linked-price assumption is a scientifically useful next boundary. The
exchange proof uses the comparison between coefficients inherited from
physical proportional loss and nonnegative capacity releases. With positive
independent objective prices, the same comparison can persist on an open
price region, but it can fail outside that region. The separate pass-21
note derives that sufficient region and gives an exact two-module,
positive-tolerance counterexample. This changes the optimization objective,
not the physical loss law or the underlying S1 contract.
