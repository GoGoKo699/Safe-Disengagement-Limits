**Pass 26 completed the common-drain question.** For two modules with one
common positive drain duration, every minimum-upkeep ready state concentrates,
even if an intermediate release helps preparation. Compactness establishes
actual attainment. The proof assumes a hypothetical two-interior minimum,
uses discounted input/initial-state perturbations to derive its scheduling
structure, then excludes it by a genuine two-sided handoff-time variation.
It includes equal coefficients, simultaneous handoffs, active caps, the release
kink and insufficient original capacity. It does not assume global seriality.
Design caps and nonnegative support charges give the nominal fixed-positive-
tolerance corollary.

The bounded primary comparison records ordinary delivery-tail and controllable-

**Pass 27 quantified a nonuniform small-mismatch boundary.** With all other
parameters fixed, an arbitrarily small positive difference between two drains
can still give a unique two-partial optimum. The exact zero-deficit excess
cost of every concentrated design is
`g_B=(y-1)^2*(y^2+2*y+3)`. For mismatch `delta=3*log(y)`, the ratio
`g_B/delta^2` tends to `2/3`. Positive preparation tolerance and normal
operating slack hold for every family member, but their certified values and
both handoff windows vanish in the common-drain limit. This is not instability
around a fixed positive preparation window or a uniform positive advantage.

The new assessment also proves an elementary duration-bound interpretation.
Independent durations bounded by `ell_i` reduce exactly to fixed maximum
durations if simultaneous maxima are admissible. Ignoring any early release
proves sufficiency. For the pass-27 family, padding both duration bounds to
their common maximum has exact zero-deficit cost `G=7-3*y^2`, exceeding
the individual-bound optimum by precisely g_B. No corresponding positive-
tolerance coarsening formula was established.

**Pass 28 extracted the general partial-order theorem.** The pair perturbations
leave every other module's recorded controls and events unchanged, and earlier
independent release only increases capacity. For any finite module count and
arbitrary independent drains, every first-hit maximal-loss witness at a minimum
therefore hands off interior partials in strictly decreasing coefficient order.
A later partial receives no input before an earlier one hands off. There is
at most one interior partial per distinct coefficient. Common coefficients
give concentration for any number of modules and arbitrary unequal drains,
including nominal fixed-positive-tolerance designs.

The order statement is not imposed on deliberately delayed ownership policies
or every smaller-loss realization. Cold and design-bound coordinates may
still interleave. In the two-module model, both coefficient and drain
heterogeneity are needed for the displayed two-partial failures; that is not
an all-dimensional characterization. Unequal coefficients with three or more
common-drain modules remain unresolved.
