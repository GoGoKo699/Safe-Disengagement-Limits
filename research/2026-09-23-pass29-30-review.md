## 1. Pass 29: all-policy separation

The displayed state `(3/100,1/8,0)` has linked upkeep `37/200`. Its two
positive coordinates lie strictly below their physical caps. Direct
substitution checks both lower-module terminal equations and the cold C
terminal balance; all handoffs and releases have the claimed event order.
Full-rate nondecreasing inputs and the strict rate barriers exclude hidden
intermediate cap crossings. Replaying the maximal-loss witness under
smaller losses supplies the robust guarantee.

The key lower-bound step is a genuine all-policy reduction. For every
state below the comparison budget, all coordinates are subfull. C's loss
rate at its cap exceeds available capacity while either A's or B's
reservation is still retained. Therefore C must hand off strictly after
both release events. The equality `D=2*ell` then puts both earlier
handoffs strictly before ell, when no drain can yet have released.

Both applications of the old first-completion front-loading argument are
legitimate on these constant-capacity comparison intervals. In particular,
the second application is not an unsupported induction through a generic
pending-drain schedule: its entire comparison interval lies before any
release. Deferring other inputs preserves or improves their state at the
comparison time, while earlier ownership handoffs advance their releases.
The transformation preserves the initial state and tolerates original
prewarming, preemption, parallelism and idling. This justifies the two
serial orders specifically for this fixture and budget.

The comparison with all concentrated states also checks. Full coordinates
are excluded by their individual costs. Every remaining concentrated state
under the threshold is dominated by one of the three one-positive states
at exactly that threshold. Upward readiness reduces the proof to those
three states, with two orders each. The branch conditions for all six
terminal formulas hold strictly.

All three rational lower bounds on radicals have positive exact square
differences. Substituting those bounds into the increasing cold-deficit
polynomials gives the six positive fractions printed in the companion
note. Their signs require no decimal approximation. The table therefore
excludes every concentrated state of upkeep at most `1851/10000`, not
merely serial schedules selected by a numerical search.

Compactness and closed concentration faces justify the strict restricted
minimum above that threshold. The all-full state ensures the restricted
ready set is nonempty in the zero-tolerance problem. The exhibited ready
cost is smaller by `1/10000`, so every unrestricted minimizer is
nonconcentrated. The extension of the first-gap exchange to an earlier
cold coordinate is valid with zero support charges and additionally forces
C to be cold at a global minimum. Thus both A and B are interior there.

These arguments establish the separation and the structure of minima.
They do not, by themselves, establish the exact unrestricted optimum or
uniqueness. Any later exact-optimization claim requires its separate
argument.

## 3. Open-family argument

The local closed graph of actual readiness is justified. On a compact
parameter neighborhood, inputs have a common finite bound and handoff
times have a common compact domain. Weak-star input subsequences and
uniformly convergent integrated paths retain the path constraints. The
discount kernels converge uniformly as the coefficients vary; capacity
step functions converge almost everywhere except at finitely many
limiting event times. Testing inequalities against nonnegative integrable
functions preserves the budgets. Terminal equality and varying deadline
bounds pass to the limit, and robust replay identifies actual readiness.

The graph of the finite union of concentrated physical faces is also
closed when caps vary. Thus a sequence of nearby concentrated ready states
below the fixed comparison threshold would contradict the base strict
separation. This is the required direction of semicontinuity; mere
continuity of a displayed schedule would not establish the lower bound.

For the upper bound, the fixed nominal state has strict upper-cap margins,
simple cap crossings, distinct event times and a strict deadline margin
at its worst corner. Those inequalities persist locally by continuous
dependence of the scalar flows and crossing times. Its cost remains below
the comparison threshold and below the normal spare budget. The radial
warmup therefore also persists.

The conclusion is a relative open family within the specified common-drain
interface. It does not require maintaining `D=2*ell` after perturbation:
that equality certified the base lower bound, and the closed-graph argument
carries the strict gap to nearby deadlines. No explicit neighborhood radius
or robustness against changing the service interface is claimed.

## 4. Exact zero-deficit optimum

The later exact-optimum note correctly starts from global attainment and
the already established minimum-state shape. The partial-order theorem
then fixes A before B, and the same-initial-state front-loading argument
produces the required serial witness. C's terminal inequality need not
be assumed to bind: it gives `Y<=Y_0(X)`, and the negative coefficient
of Y in the objective directly gives the scalar lower bound `L>=F(X)`.

The scalar domain deliberately relaxes the remaining physical constraints.
That relaxation is in the correct direction for a lower bound. The
derivatives of `Y_0=(K-k*X^3)^(1/3)` and F were checked independently.
On the entire relaxed interval, `X>=1` and `Y_0<51/32`; exact arithmetic
checks the cube difference `378837/218071040` and the positive lower
bound `11951206724/2515363286777` for `F''`.

The two derivative terms at `X=11/10`, `Y_0=3/2` are exactly `-39/55`
and `39/55`. Strict convexity gives the unique scalar minimum, whose
value is exactly `37/200`. Equality in the earlier objective lower bound
forces `Y=Y_0(X)`, and the first two terminal balances then uniquely
recover `(3/100,1/8,0)`. This proves a unique global initial-state
optimizer, while correctly leaving policy uniqueness unclaimed.

### Positive-deficit value argument

The exact zero-deficit result supports the sharper formula

$$C_\epsilon=37/200+3\epsilon,
\qquad 0<\epsilon\leq1/30000,$$

with unique nominal optimizer `x*+(epsilon,epsilon,0)`. The argument
was checked separately from the simpler separation proof in Section 2.

The displayed nominal point has worst corner x* and cost at most the
concentrated comparison budget throughout this interval. At any nominal
minimum, no coordinate can lie in `(0,epsilon]`, since deleting it leaves
the same worst corner and lowers upkeep. No coordinate is nominally full,
because its individual full cost exceeds that budget. The nominal state
cannot be concentrated, since it is also exactly ready. Thus it and its
effective corner have the same support of at least two positive
coordinates.

For coefficients `(2,1,3)`, every such support has coefficient sum at
least three. The exact accounting identity
`L(p)=L(q)+epsilon*sum_{q_i>0}gamma_i`, together with `L(q)>=J`, yields
`L(p)>=J+3*epsilon`. Equality forces `L(q)=J` and hence `q=x*` by the
unique zero-deficit optimizer. This gives the asserted nominal equality
case directly. No support charge is dropped, and no additional
cold-coordinate exchange is needed. All effective design-cap constraints
retain strict margin at `epsilon=1/30000`, so the endpoint is included.

The strictly earlier nominal witness used for the parameter-neighborhood
argument is a different, slightly more expensive target. An exact optimum
whose effective corner is x* need not have strict deadline slack; the
review does not substitute that endpoint witness into the open-family
argument.
