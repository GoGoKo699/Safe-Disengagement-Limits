# Cold startup at the critical maintenance budget

Date: 22 September 2026. This follow-on pass distinguishes finite paid warmup
from the initialized recurring-upkeep theorem. It gives an exact two-module
result and a counterexample to the tempting assertion that equality in the
maintenance budget rules out starting a sustainable ready deployment from cold.
Publication novelty and a concrete operational realization remain unresolved.

## 1. Question and assumptions

Consider two independent modules with `M_1,M_2>0`, `a_1,a_2>=0`, and **strictly
positive** invalidation bounds `d_1,d_2>0`. Use the fixed-rate reflected dynamics
of the active model and the critical normal spare budget

$$s=d_1+d_2.$$

Both modules start cold. Throughout warmup they continue the prescribed normal
service; no module transfers to fallback, so available preparation capacity is
the fixed `s`. Optional work may be zero. The warmup may last any finite time
and is paid in full. A deadline guarantee is required only **after** warmup.
Requesting the same guarantee from the initial cold instant is a different,
stronger contract and is not claimed here.

Analyze reachability first under the allowed simultaneous maximum history
`rho_i=d_i`. The constructive policies are robust in the useful sense that
smaller actual invalidation produces componentwise at least as much preparation
after the same prescribed warmup. Extra useful preparation cannot make an exit
harder. Receiver resources, essential service, instantaneous cutovers, and
capacity release have the same explicit meaning as in the other current notes.

Define

$$M_{\max}=\max(M_1,M_2),\qquad M_{\min}=\min(M_1,M_2),
\qquad B_i=a_i+d_i.$$

Both `B_i` are positive under the assumptions.

## 2. Exact normal-operation reachable set

**Theorem 1.** Under maximum invalidation, the states reachable in finite time
from cold, without any normal-mode transfer, are exactly

$$\mathcal R=
\{(p_1,p_2):0\leq p_i\leq M_i,\ p_1+p_2\leq M_{\max}\}.$$

**Necessity.** Write `Q=p_1+p_2`. If `Q>M_max`, both coordinates must be positive,
because neither coordinate alone can exceed `M_max`. On that set the reflected
dynamics and the budget give

$$Q'\leq v_1+v_2-d_1-d_2\leq0.$$

The positive part `(Q-M_max)_+` is absolutely continuous and has nonpositive
derivative almost everywhere where it is positive; on its zero set its
derivative is zero almost everywhere. Starting from zero, it therefore remains
zero. Equivalently, once both coordinates are positive, total preparation cannot
increase; at any boundary where one is zero it is at most `M_max`.

**Sufficiency.** Let the target be `p` in the displayed set and let
`Q=p_1+p_2`. Choose a larger-size module `L` with `M_L=M_max`, and let `J` be the
other module. First allocate all `s` to `L`, preparing exactly `Q` there. Its
net rate is `s-d_L=d_J>0`, so this takes `Q/d_J` time.

Next allocate all `s` to `J` for `p_J/d_L` time. While both coordinates are
positive, the donor `L` loses preparation at rate `d_L` and the recipient `J`
gains it at the same rate `s-d_J=d_L`. This starts from `(Q,0)` in `(L,J)`
coordinates and ends at `(p_L,p_J)`. All intermediate states stay in their
per-module bounds: `L` decreases from at most `M_L` to a nonnegative target,
and `J` increases to at most `M_J`. Zero durations and a zero donor at the final
instant are harmless. Both phases take finite time. This proves reachability.

The proof needs `d_1,d_2>0`. If, for example, `d_1=s>0` and `d_2=0`, module 1
cannot grow from zero under maximum invalidation, so the triangle is not the
reachable set. That degenerate family requires a separate statement.

Every state in `R` can be held indefinitely at the critical budget: allocate
`v_i=d_i` to positive coordinates and zero to cold ones. At maximum invalidation
the state is constant; at smaller invalidation it cannot decrease. An interior
state with both positive coordinates uses all spare capacity, leaving optional
rate zero. This is a sustainable state, not merely a state one can pass through.

## 3. Exact earliest exit deadline achievable after warmup

Let `H_warm` be the smallest robust removal deadline that can be maintained
after some finite cold-start warmup under this fixed normal load, with no
transfers during warmup. Both startup and the subsequent ready operation are
subject to the original budget; initial useful preparation is not granted free.

**Theorem 2.**

$$H_{\rm warm}=\frac{M_{\min}}{\max(B_1,B_2)}
=\frac{\min(M_1,M_2)}{\max(a_1+d_1,a_2+d_2)}.$$

**Lower bound over arbitrary post-request schedules.** Follow any strategy
under maximum invalidation. Until its first transfer, preparation capacity is
still `s`, so the invariant `Q<=M_max` continues to hold even after the request.
If module `i` transfers first, then `p_i=M_i`; the remaining module `j` can have
at most `M_max-M_i` useful preparation. Its remaining deficit is therefore at
least

$$M_j-(M_{\max}-M_i)=M_{\min}.$$

After this transfer the maximum net growth of that remaining coordinate is

$$s+a_i-d_j=a_i+d_i=B_i.$$

It thus needs at least `M_min/B_i` additional time. The time already spent
before the first transfer is nonnegative. This proves

$$T\geq\frac{M_{\min}}{B_i}
\geq\frac{M_{\min}}{\max(B_1,B_2)}.$$

The bound allows arbitrary preemption, parallel allocation, and loss of
preparation. Simultaneous first completions are impossible in this family,
because both full preparations would have sum `M_1+M_2>M_max`.

**Attaining construction.** Select `i` maximizing `B_i`, and prepare the state

$$p_i=M_i,\qquad p_j=M_{\max}-M_i.$$

It belongs to `R`, so Theorem 1 gives finite cold-start warmup. Keep it ready by
the maintenance policy described above. At a request, transfer `i` immediately.
The remaining preparation deficit of `j` is exactly `M_min`; allocating all
`s+a_i` to `j` completes exit in `M_min/B_i`. Lower invalidation cannot delay
either warmup preparedness or exit. The deadline is attained, including equality.

Thus a deadline `H` can be maintained after such warmup if and only if
`H>=H_warm`. This is an exact result for the stated two-module critical-budget
family, not a general heterogeneous startup formula.

## 4. Why fully ready subset startup gives the wrong answer

A nonempty subset `S` can be brought from cold to **all members fully ready**
under a fixed normal preparation budget `s` only if

$$s>\sum_{i\in S}d_i.$$

This strict condition is also sufficient if startup need not preserve a tight
exit guarantee: choose positive extras `epsilon_i` with
`sum_{i in S}(d_i+epsilon_i)<=s`, allocate those rates to unfinished members,
and maintain completed ones at `d_i`.

For necessity when `s<=sum_{i in S}d_i`, consider the final connected interval
before supposed completion on which all members of `S` have positive
preparation. On that interval their total preparation cannot increase. At its
left endpoint at least one member was zero, so its sum was strictly less than
`sum_{i in S}M_i`. It cannot reach the all-full sum. Extra preparation outside
`S` cannot help this budget inequality. The argument includes zero invalidation
rates and the zero-capacity case; the subset must be nonempty and all its sizes
strictly positive.

But the recurring-upkeep theorem minimizes over optimistic **fully ready
subsets** to characterize long-run cost. Its attaining construction is not a
claim that every sustainable policy must use fully ready members only.
Partially prepared stationary states can be easier to reach from cold while
having exactly the same upkeep.

Suppose, for clarity, `M_1>M_2` and `B_2>B_1`. The two proper nonempty ready
subsets have exact exit times

$$\tau(\{1\})=M_2/B_1,\qquad
\tau(\{2\})=M_1/B_2.$$

For every deadline in the nonempty interval

$$\frac{M_2}{B_2}\leq H<
\min\left(\frac{M_2}{B_1},\frac{M_1}{B_2}\right),$$

no proper ready subset meets the deadline, so the exact recurring cost is
`D(H)=d_1+d_2=s`. The all-full state is unreachable from cold by Theorem 1, yet
the sustainable partially prepared state

$$p_1=M_1-M_2,\qquad p_2=M_2$$

is reachable and meets the deadline. This is not a counterexample to the upkeep
reduction: both the lower bound and the partial policy charge the same `D(H)`.
It is a counterexample to extending its fully-ready attaining construction into
an unwarranted startup impossibility claim.

## 5. Exact rational witness

Use

$$s=1,\quad d_1=d_2=\tfrac12,\quad
(M_1,M_2)=(2,1),\quad(a_1,a_2)=(1,3),\quad H=\tfrac12.$$

The cold normal warmup is:

| Phase | Duration | Allocation `(v_1,v_2)` | Ending preparation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Prepare module 1 | `4` | `(1,0)` | `(2,0)` |
| Redistribute to module 2 | `2` | `(0,1)` | `(1,1)` |
| Ready normal operation | indefinite | `(1/2,1/2)` | `(1,1)` |

Every rate and endpoint in the table is for maximum invalidation. Smaller
invalidation gives at least these useful preparations under the same schedule.
No tight exit guarantee is claimed during the six-unit warmup.

At any later request, module 2 transfers instantly. Capacity rises to `4` and
module 1 has one unit of remaining preparation. Its net rate is `4-1/2=7/2`,
so exit takes `2/7`, strictly below `H=1/2`.

The ready/cold subset comparison is exact:

| Initially ready subset | Exact optimal exit time | Meets `H=1/2`? |
|---|---:|---|
| Empty | `18/7` | No |
| `{1}` | `2/3` | No |
| `{2}` | `4/7` | No |
| `{1,2}` | `0` | Yes |

Hence `D(H)=1=s`. The all-full state `(2,1)` is inaccessible from cold since
every reachable state has `Q<=2`, but the stationary partially prepared state
`(1,1)` is accessible and meets the deadline. The exact best warmup-ready
deadline is `H_warm=2/7`.

## 6. Status and remaining scope

These statements have complete analytic proofs in the restricted two-module
family. Exact finite checks in `analysis/verify_heterogeneous.py` support the
rational witness, reachable-set construction, and threshold identities; those
checks do not substitute for the continuous-time necessity proofs above.

The result sharpens the interpretation of paid initialization: fully ready
subsets suffice for the best long-run upkeep, while reachable partial states
can be essential when startup is constrained to the prescribed normal budget.
It does not justify an immediately available guarantee at cold time zero and
does not establish a real application or a new general theorem of standby
maintenance. A prior-art audit of constrained reachability and cold-start
readiness remains necessary before treating it as a paper contribution.
