## All-policy lower bound and equality

The earliest possible drain completion is `log(10/3)`, strictly later than
the larger required handoff deadline `log3`, because `9<10`. Thus every
feasible policy has preparation capacity four throughout its relevant
handoff interval. Positive released rates of any magnitude cannot change
that conclusion. Initially full states, delayed ownership handoff, and
simultaneous handoffs are included.

The reflected-waste term in the integrated scalar equation has the correct
nonnegative sign. Dropping it preserves the lower bound even for a policy
that reaches a cap before handing off. In the B-first case, `t_B<=h_A`
implies initial B preparation at least `29/10`, already more expensive than
the candidate total upkeep.

For A first, the discounted input weights satisfy `2*exp(2t)>exp(t)` at
every nonnegative time. Applying the shared capacity bound before and after
A's handoff gives

$$L(p)\geq4-3e^{2t_A}+4e^{t_A}-e^{t_B}.$$

Its derivatives in both times are strictly negative. Substituting the two
separate deadlines gives the lower bound `177/100`. Equality requires both
deadlines to be tight, all input assigned to A before its handoff and to B
afterward, and zero reflected waste on the maximal-loss realization. The
integrated equations then fix the initial state uniquely as
`(37/200,7/5)`. No serial optimality theorem, control discretization, or
unproved attainment claim is used.

## Attainment, normal operation, and precision

The displayed preparation paths stay below their caps until the planned
handoffs. B's waiting value at A's handoff is `14/11`, followed by a monotone
increase to three. Both drains finish at `log10`. Under smaller losses,
comparison and full-rate holding at an early cap preserve those planned
handoffs, since four exceeds both full loss rates. The interface explicitly
keeps preparation loss active until ownership is handed off, then ends it
while the immutable drain retains the separate source reservation.

The optimum has normal upkeep strictly below four. Allocations `(1,3)`
from cold for `log2` reach `(3/8,3/2)`, which strictly dominates the optimum.
Its maintenance allocation preserves that domination and leaves optional
rate `223/100`. The total-storage inequality supplies the matching
long-run upper bound for arbitrary normal policies.

For fixed positive request-time tolerance, scalar deadline bounds force
both effective coordinates to be positive: `q_A>=37/200` and `q_B>=1`.
There is consequently no omitted zero-support branch when writing
`L(p)=L(q)+3*epsilon`. The unique effective minimum remains the same, giving

$$C_\epsilon^{\rm drain}=177/100+3\epsilon
\quad(0\leq\epsilon\leq63/200).$$

Above that interval A's nominal cap violates its scalar bound. The nominal
minimum has two partial coordinates for every strict interior value of
epsilon, and even at the terminal precision its upkeep is only `543/200`,
leaving positive optional capacity. This is the specified one-time deficit
contract; it is not a claim about arbitrary additional ongoing disturbances.

## Surviving subclass and attribution boundary

For a common drain duration with `ell<=H<=2*ell`, no release can affect any
handoff due by `H-ell`. The note's exact reduction to the zero-release
instantaneous model checks in both directions, including equality at
`H=2*ell` because inputs have no impulses. Thus positive drain time by
itself is not enough to infer concentration failure.

The unequal-drain witness introduces two distinct preparation deadlines.
Its discounted-integral lower bound is an elementary allocation argument.
The result establishes an assumption boundary in this repository; it does
not establish novelty against scheduling with delivery tails, deadlines, or
resource-dependent processing.
