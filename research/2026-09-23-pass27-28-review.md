## 1. Small unequal-drain family

The [pass-27 family](2026-09-23-pass27-small-drain-mismatch.md) preserves
the pass-25 interface and fixes every parameter other than the two drain
durations. The range `1<y<=11/10` gives `y^8<10`, so no reservation can
release before either required handoff. The initial state is strictly
partial on both coordinates.

The A-first discounted-input certificate is valid for arbitrary controls,
including reflection and delayed handoff. Its strictly decreasing time
bound is minimized at the two separate handoff deadlines. The B-first
certificate is also correct: bounding both phases by A's larger discounted
weight gives `G=7-3*y^2`, and

$$G-C=y^4-4y+3=(y-1)^2(y^2+2y+3)>0.$$

Equality conditions in the A-first case force the stated unique initial
state. The explicit full-rate schedule attains it under maximal loss, and
comparison with holding at an early cap proves the guarantee under every
smaller allowed loss history.

The exact best concentrated cost follows from the two scalar initial-state
bounds. Neither coordinate can be cold, so one must be full. Both full-A
and full-B candidates have explicit attaining schedules; the full-B branch
is cheaper because `(y^2-1)*(y^2-2)<0`. Thus the concentrated penalty is
exactly `g_B`, not merely the gap to one selected competitor.

For precision, both effective coordinates must be positive. The identity
`L(p)=L(q)+3*epsilon` therefore has no missing support case. The margin
comparison

$$2(g_A-g_B)=(y-1)(9+y-2y^2-2y^3)>0$$

is correct, including the displayed lower bracket bound `2459/500`.
It establishes the exact nominal minimum through `epsilon=g_B`, positive
normal slack throughout that interval, and two partial nominal coordinates
strictly below its endpoint. The note correctly leaves larger epsilon
unresolved; a cap binding at the old optimum does not imply general
infeasibility. The finite cold-warmup construction respects the original
normal budget and makes no promise during warmup.

Finally, the exact expansions and the limiting ratio `2/3` check. As the
drain mismatch tends to zero, the two available preparation windows both
collapse. The guaranteed tolerance, upkeep slack and concentration penalty
also vanish. The family consequently establishes neither a fixed positive
advantage nor instability near a fixed common-drain instance with a
positive preparation window.

The additional bounded-duration corollary also checks. If independent drain
durations lie in `[0,ell_i]` and their maximum durations are jointly
admissible, robust readiness equals readiness in the fixed-maximum-duration
model: necessity uses the joint maximum history, while sufficiency replays
its policy with conservative release timers and ignores earlier available
capacity. Independence and immutable drains are essential to this replay.
For pass 27, padding both bounds to `ell_A` imposes the common handoff
deadline `h_A`. The B-first lower bound G is attained by full B at time zero
and the stated A preparation. The A-first bound is at least
`W(h_A,h_A)=G+3*(y-1)>G`. Thus the exact zero-deficit upkeep cost of this
coarser common bound is `G-C=g_B` for this family. This does not supply a
positive-tolerance cost formula or a general formula for bound coarsening.

## 2. Arbitrary-module partial ordering

The [pass-28 theorem](2026-09-23-pass28-partial-order.md) correctly extends
the pair improvements without assuming that either coordinate is globally
first. Its compactness argument retains the individual handoff deadlines,
all intermediate caps, and actual robust replay. Finite n supplies only
finitely many capacity jumps; weak-star input limits and uniform integrated
path limits preserve the constraints. Nonnegative support charges ensure
lower semicontinuity and attainment.

In the first-gap advance, A becomes full earlier and B's state is decreased
until A's old handoff, then exactly restored. Every other recorded input,
state, handoff and drain end remains unchanged. Reassigning A's removed
input to B preserves total preparation use; A's own reservation releases
earlier, which can only increase available capacity. This argument works
with intervening other handoffs, different drain lengths, and initially
full or cold other modules. The strict upkeep reduction for an earlier
no-faster interior coordinate is therefore valid.

For a faster earlier interior coordinate, transferring any positive-measure
portion of the later coordinate's input to it, with the matching discounted
initial changes, preserves total input pointwise and both handoff times.
Strict initial design-cap room and a compact pre-handoff cap margin justify
the perturbation. The linked exponential weights give a strict cost
reduction. Hence the later interior coordinate receives zero input almost
everywhere before the earlier one hands off.

The theorem states these properties for **every first-hit maximal-loss
witness at a minimum**. It explicitly avoids imposing them on deliberately
delayed ownership policies or every smaller-loss realization. This is the
correct quantifier for the first-gap proof. Equal-coefficient pairs,
including simultaneous handoffs, are excluded. Thus the distinct-coefficient
bound and arbitrary-module common-coefficient concentration follow.

The positive-tolerance conversion to effective design caps and fixed
support charges is exact. Local improvements keep both coordinates
positive, so those charges do not alter the argument. Concentration in
that corollary concerns nominal preparation; effective cap values do not
become physical ownership thresholds.

Cold and design-capped coordinates can still interrupt preparation or
release capacity. Neither proof gives a general serial scheduler or
settles arbitrary unequal-coefficient, multi-module concentration. No new
finite checker was added: these general quantifiers rest on the complete
written arguments, not a parameter grid or numerical control search.
