## A generic separation is elementary

The following scalar benchmark is an internal deduction, not a result
attributed to a newly inspected source. Let `x(0)=0` and

$$\dot x=1-x,\qquad 0\le x\le1,\qquad
\mathcal R=[1/2,1],\qquad L(x)=2-x,\qquad s=1.$$

The unique trajectory is `x(t)=1-exp(-t)`. It enters the closed region R
at `t=log 2` and remains there forever. The minimum of L on R is `s=1`,
uniquely at `x*=1`, which is never reached at a finite time. Moreover,
the nonnegative storage `Q=1-x` satisfies

$$\dot Q=-(L(x)-s),\qquad
\int_{\log2}^{\infty}(L(x(t))-s)\,dt=1/2.$$

Thus finite entry into a viable region, critical dissipation, convergence
to a unique cost minimum, and failure of finite arrival can coexist in a
one-dimensional elementary system. Those abstract facts are not a new
principle of safe disengagement.

This is **not** an S1 instance: its assigned cost decreases with the scalar
state, and it has neither the module-specific proportional upkeep nor a
deadline region generated by S1's post-request schedule. It does not refute
the pass-15 two-module boundary. It prevents overclaiming the generic
viability-versus-finite-reachability distinction while leaving the specific
S1 geometry to be assessed.
