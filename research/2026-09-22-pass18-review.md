## Findings

No mathematical correction was required in the reviewed versions. The
following checks were done independently of the drafting argument.

1. **Pass-17 fixed-order geometry.** For the displayed two-module instance,
   the stage equations give the boundary `y=3*sqrt(3-2*x)-4`. Both proposed
   rational endpoints lie on it. Their midpoint would require
   `7578/400 <= 7569/400` after squaring two positive quantities, so the
   fixed-order ready region is nonconvex. The convexity comparison excludes
   the stated affine identifications only; the note retains this restriction.
2. **Pass-17 common-clock obstruction.** Differentiation with respect to
   preparation forces `f'(z)*exp(-gamma*z)` to be constant on overlapping
   rays. Differentiation with respect to start time then contradicts
   `b/(b-gamma*M)>1`. The proof needs only the stated `C^1` clock with
   positive derivative, and does not exclude schedule-dependent encodings.
3. **One-sided oracle.** Every positive-deficit corner is strictly subfull,
   so the same finite family of strictly accessible permutations applies
   for every positive epsilon. Each stage recurrence is continuous on the
   entire closed preparation box. This proves the finite-minimum limit,
   including the infinite case when cold exit is blocked. Delayed initially
   full coordinates correctly undergo waiting decay.
4. **Maintainability and concentration.** The accessible-full-prefix
   construction proves equality of ordinary and limiting costs whenever
   `C(H)<s`. If `C_-(H)<=s`, a strict inequality `C(H)<C_-(H)` would fall
   into that strict-slack case and contradict equality. Thus every limiting
   minimizer in the maintainable regime is an ordinary minimizer, and the
   established every-minimizer concentration theorem applies. No extension
   to `C_->s` or to fixed-positive-tolerance minimizers is inferred.
5. **Positive-tolerance limit.** For cold-finite systems and `H>0`, moving
   a limiting optimum slightly toward the all-full vector creates strict
   time slack whenever it is needed. An attaining strict-order completion
   time decreases strictly under that movement. Continuity then admits a
   sufficiently small positive deficit, proving `C_epsilon -> C_-`.
   The exclusion of `H=0` is necessary, since no positive deficit permits
   instantaneous completion of all modules.
6. **General positive-tolerance margin.** The added bound
   `C_epsilon(H)>=C(H)+gamma_min*epsilon`, when `C(H)>0`, is correct.
   Any feasible degraded state `q=(p-epsilon)_+` has positive upkeep, hence
   some `q_i>0`; that coordinate alone adds `gamma_i*epsilon` to nominal
   upkeep. Taking the infimum proves the bound, including an infeasible
   left side. The storage argument consequently excludes indefinite
   readiness for every originally critical case `C=s>0` at any positive
   tolerance under the stated request-time perturbation contract.
7. **Exact critical fixture.** Independent substitution yields
   `exp(F_-(p))=4-4*p_A-p_B`, original unique optimum `(0,1)` of cost one,
   and limiting unique optimum `(1/2,2/3)` of cost `7/6`. For positive
   tolerance, both degraded coordinates must be positive. Minimizing the
   resulting linear inequality gives the nominal optimum
   `(1/2,2/3+5*epsilon)` and cost `7/6+5*epsilon` precisely for
   `0<epsilon<=1/15`. Above that threshold even the maximal nominal vector
   fails. The one-module continuous formula confirms minimal dimension
   under the finite-cold-exit requirement.

## Interpretation and remaining limits

The new perturbation is explicitly a one-time bounded preparation deficit
at the request, charged against a nominal maintained state. It is not a
continuous stream of extra losses or an experimentally calibrated error
model. The lower bound on nominal upkeep therefore applies to the exact
contract that was defined, without silently changing normal loss dynamics.

The general positive-tolerance margin already destroys exact critical
feasibility. The fixture's additional content is its nonvanishing limiting
cost jump of `1/6`; the paper now distinguishes these two facts. Strict
optional slack survives sufficiently small positive tolerance, but neither
exact finite-tolerance equality of optimal rates nor concentration of
finite-tolerance minimizers is claimed.

General concentration for `C_->s` remains open in this pass. In particular,
an initially full module can lie between two partial modules in a strict
permutation and decay before service, so the existing constant cold-block
exchange proof cannot simply be copied. This limitation does not affect
the proved maintainable regime.
