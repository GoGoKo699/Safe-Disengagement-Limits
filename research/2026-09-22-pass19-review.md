## Independent derivation

Fix a strictly accessible order. For an intermediate module whose initial
preparation `q` is held fixed, its completion map as a function of global
start time is

$$\phi(t)=\frac1\gamma\log\frac{b e^{\gamma t}-\gamma q}{b-\gamma M},
\qquad b>\gamma M,\quad 0\leq q\leq M.$$

The denominator and logarithm argument are positive for every `t>=0`.
Direct differentiation gives

$$\phi'(t)=\frac{b e^{\gamma t}}{b e^{\gamma t}-\gamma q}\geq1,
\qquad
\phi''(t)=-\gamma\phi'(t)(\phi'(t)-1)\leq0.$$

Also `phi(0)>=0`. An empty block gives the identity map; any nonempty
composition `G` of these maps is increasing, twice continuously
differentiable and concave, with `G(0)>=0` and `G'>=1`. The tangent inequality
for a concave function therefore gives the essential anchored bound

$$G(t)\geq G(0)+tG'(t)\geq tG'(t),\qquad t\geq0.$$

Now choose two preparation coordinates strictly inside their allowed
positive intervals. Write their decay coefficients as `alpha,beta`. Keep
the earlier module's start time and the later module's completion time
fixed; vary the earlier completion time `t`. Keep every intervening initial
preparation fixed. The varied pair's linked upkeep, including any constant
nominal-preparation offsets, has the form

$$c(t)=\text{constant}-D_j e^{\alpha t}+b_k e^{\beta G(t)},
\qquad D_j=b_j-\alpha M_j>0.$$

Nonnegative releases imply `b_k>=b_j>D_j`. At a stationary point, an
inequality `beta*G'(t)>=alpha` would, by the anchored bound and `t>=0`, imply

$$b_k\beta G'(t)e^{\beta G(t)}
\geq b_k\alpha e^{\alpha t}
>D_j\alpha e^{\alpha t},$$

contradicting `c'(t)=0`. Hence every stationary point satisfies
`beta*G'(t)<alpha`. Substitution of the stationarity identity into the
second derivative yields

$$c''(t)=\alpha D_j e^{\alpha t}
\left[\beta G'(t)+\frac{G''(t)}{G'(t)}-\alpha\right]<0.$$

There is therefore no local minimum with two interior preparation
coordinates. No comparison between the two decay coefficients is assumed.

## Feasible variation and endpoint checks

The earlier interior preparation is strictly below its physical threshold,
so its completion time is strictly positive. Its preparation is a smooth,
strictly decreasing function of the varied completion time. The later
preparation is a smooth, strictly increasing function of that same time.
Both start strictly inside their allowed intervals, so sufficiently small
variations in both directions remain feasible. All intermediate schedules
are actual strictly accessible stages, and the unchanged later completion
time leaves the entire suffix unchanged. These facts justify the local
stationarity and second-order necessary conditions used above.

Intermediate preparations may be zero, physically full, or partial. A
physically full module which waits is correctly represented by its
completion map and waiting decay. Neither adjacency of the two varied
modules nor a cold-only intervening block is required. An earlier cold
prefix merely changes the additive constant in `c(t)`; it does not change
the fact that `t` is a nonnegative global time.

For fixed positive tolerance, put `q_i=(p_i-epsilon)_+`. At a nominal
minimum, `0<p_i<=epsilon` is impossible: setting that coordinate to zero
leaves `q` unchanged and strictly decreases nominal upkeep. Thus every
nominally partial coordinate corresponds to
`0<q_i<M_i-epsilon`, and its nominal upkeep differs from `gamma_i*q_i`
by the constant `gamma_i*epsilon` on its active support. The exchange applies
without assuming equal coefficients. If `epsilon>=M_i`, optimal nominal
preparation for that coordinate is zero. An empty feasible set has no
minimizer; otherwise the finite strict-order family and compact nominal
box give attainment. A positive deadline is not required for the structural
claim itself, although the positive-tolerance feasible set at deadline zero
is empty for a positive number of modules.

For the limiting oracle, take `q=p` and the physical bounds directly. The
same proof applies to every finite-cost minimizer, including costs above
the normal budget. This resolves the specific unrestricted-concentration
gap retained in pass 18.

## Verdict and scope

The argument proves an **every-minimizer** statement, because any supposed
minimum with two partial coordinates supplies an attaining strict order
and then violates a necessary condition for a local minimum along its
feasible exchange curve. It proves concentration both for the limiting
oracle at every budget and for fixed positive preparation tolerance with
unequal positive proportional coefficients.

The hypotheses used are positive thresholds and decay coefficients,
nonnegative capacity releases, strict accessibility of the chosen order,
and upkeep prices linked to the decay coefficients. This review does not
extend the result to arbitrary prices or nonlinear losses. It also does
not transfer the earlier exact-frontier algorithm or its complexity to the
new tolerance problem without a separate derivation.

The exact prepared-middle illustration added to the full pass-19 note was
also checked independently. At `z=25/16`, substitution gives `q_1=15/128`,
`q_3=1/2` and nominal upkeep `971/320`. The variable upkeep is a quadratic

it supplies no global minimum-cost certificate, as the note correctly states.
